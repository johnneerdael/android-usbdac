## Transport

- **1.1 and Q04.** Preserve exact mode and the proven UAC1 Type I refusal. A retained owner can try a different rate after complete, read-only evidence that the current candidate cannot be carried, or after proven restoration. Claim failures, I/O errors and uncertainty about hardware mutation stay terminal. The QEMU evidence on `50b005a` covers 44.1-to-48 kHz UAC1 and reconfiguration rollback. UAC2 with the same PCM shape at different rates requires its own proof. [QEMU retry](https://github.com/johnneerdael/android-usbdac/actions/runs/46057550570).
- **1.4 and native ownership.** A USB descriptor witness may perform standard synchronous reads on an attachment. Any retained libusb context that runs events requires exclusive event ownership. Transfer slots, clocks and hand-back leases must keep the owner alive through retirement, including unwinding. Packet geometry is checked before submission, and completion cleanup cannot release a later submission.
- **2.1 and C01.** A tolerated missing implicit-feedback schedule must still rearm one OUT slot. Synthetic timing uses previously proven cadence and writes silence without consuming source frames, setting lock or extending the observation lifetime. A second gap before proof returns remains outside the bounded recovery policy. ALSA uses zero packet sizes to request nominal sizing and has a device quirk for all-empty completions. [ALSA endpoint scheduling](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/sound/usb/endpoint.c#L1790-L1846).
- **2.6 and T04.** Distinguish loop elapsed time, time outside the event pump, intentional flush cancellation and age since callback publication. The callback timestamp begins inside libusb dispatch. It cannot measure when hardware completed a packet or prove when the kernel woke the thread.
- **Q01–Q03, T01–T06, X01.** Preserve conservative clock evidence, lower-first fallback, bounded feedback intake and loss recovery. Document where UAC3 rejects ambiguous ranges and how lower-first ranking can reduce output bandwidth. Default adaptive queue geometry reaches 30 ms at 125 µs and 32 ms at longer service intervals. [ALSA range enumeration](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/c03c1a14507dfaf8b856c2608f9ac216021fc222/sound/usb/format.c#L441-L480), [USB Audio 2.0 formats bundle](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/Audio2.0_final.zip), [USB 2.0 specification](https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-20-specification).

## Routing and release

- **2.2, R05 and R06.** Prove the complete edit plan before route writes. Budget forward and recovery I/O against finite request limits, with separate power restoration. Preserve the first transaction error and a result for every attempted key, including failed readback. A ledger can contain 1,024 keys across transactions, while an individual edit batch is limited to 32.
- **R01 and R11.** Keep USB-IF mixer addresses. A same-address readback cannot independently identify a physical crosspoint. DJM request completion is an acknowledgement. Vendor modes remain fenced by the release policy.
- **Reconfiguration and Kotlin close.** Busy is immediate while a competing reconfiguration drains. A terminal failure requiring close leaves the owner fenced. A stream close can return while the USB worker still owns cancelled transfers. Kotlin records both hand-back decisions on the native owner. Native retirement restores eligible volume and routing before releasing the device to Android.
- **2.3, P03 and P04.** Preserve immutable source, workflow, run, attempt and artifact binding. Reuse a failed reservation for changed bytes only if there is evidence publication never began and registry absence is verified. Same-source recovery can reuse a digest-proven artifact. A failed initial build is not evidence that a later release of the version failed.
- **O04.** Keep `Encoding` exhaustive in 0.1.x. Adding a variant or `#[non_exhaustive]` needs a communicated compatibility boundary and migration guidance. [Cargo compatibility rules](https://doc.rust-lang.org/cargo/reference/semver.html#major-adding-non_exhaustive-to-an-existing-enum-variant-or-struct-with-no-private-fields).

## Android and conversion

- **1.2 and 1.3.** Cached capability answers distinguish direct descriptor admission, paths that need processing, unknown support and known refusal. Legacy custom sources and an attached USB target with an unknown mode list can reach the open path. Media3 requests direct float decoder output only for a direct float verdict. Attached, identity-bound USB capability data comes before the managed fallback rules. [Media3 1.11.1 AudioSink](https://github.com/androidx/media/blob/1.11.1/libraries/exoplayer/src/main/java/androidx/media3/exoplayer/audio/AudioSink.java).
- **2.4 and 2.5.** Android DSD conversion can choose higher-precision PCM when a backend and route admit it. Share alternate admission across Kotlin callers and verify it against the real native calculation. A carrier or PCM mode alone cannot prove that a downstream DAC supports the source format.
- **D01–D06.** Keep explicit headroom and clip reporting. Assess DSD quality at the final PCM target because the final resampler can reject an alias from the intermediate stage. Preserve whole-frame tail delivery and DoP marker continuity. The sink must distinguish losses after conversion from direct transport guarantees.
- **A01–A06 and X03.** Record automatic PCM conversion and consented DoP unpacking in migration guidance. Classify Media3 write recovery according to whether the source can recreate the output and resume correctly. Test the float decoder query and retained-owner reconfiguration entry points. Claims about clicks need a measured capture.

### Decisions on the smaller findings

- A callback with unavailable status loses delivery evidence. The transport conservatively charges that completion and clears its bit-perfect claim. The cleanup guards ensure the next submitted transfer keeps its own accounting.
- `SET_IFACE_FIRST` avoids the pre-alternate clock snapshot on supported quirk paths. A retry that would require restoring the old clock depends on that snapshot. Imported `GET_SAMPLE_RATE` admission stays confined to UAC1. An explicit host setting that disables required verification has its own compatibility scope.
- DoP validation work is bounded to the accepted batch. The parser can restart at an independently framed track boundary while downstream pending audio stays queued. Direct DoP maintains continuous wire marker phase.
- The native converter checks destination writability on each call. The existing read-only-buffer and retry cases exercise it. Resolving the method faster is an optimization that would require performance evidence.
- The production USB link calls the native candidate coordinator once per request. Its fallback decision depends on a clean refusal attributable to the retained owner. An implementation without that proof cannot safely retry an arbitrary failed candidate.
- The DSD tail copy retains pending whole frames until the target accepts them. The actual converter's tail tests and the host-JNI partial-write test preserve duration and byte order.

### Previously unreviewed source paths

- **UAC1 status and UAC2 live alternates.** The UAC1 path requires one owning control collection and rechecks active alternate and sample rate before optional acknowledgements. The UAC2 replacement path pins the live endpoint, packet geometry, feedback relationship and clock route. Existing tests exercise notice ambiguity, detach, changed rate and incompatible alternate proofs.
- **ADC3 discovery, power and clock transactions.** Candidate construction checks the function, format and endpoint geometry. The power lease snapshots state, verifies function peers are idle before mutations and restores clock controls before releasing domain power. Read-only rate exclusions are limited to proven direct unpowered routes. Existing tests exercise power and clock rollback through recording I/O.
- **Duplex and capture.** Capture registration binds advertised identity, active configuration and owner claims. Feedforward publishes data from submissions made after timing confirmation. Duplex rollback rechecks peer ownership and restores frequency only where current state proves the write is still owned. Independent capture retirement requires separate direct clock roots and separate AudioControl functions.
- **Audio status and JNI registry.** Capture-only fault attribution requires genuinely independent watches. Revalidation is bounded and stale device handles fail closed. JNI serializes capture insertion against DAC removal, then closes registered captures before final DAC retirement. The public capture API still relies on caller-exclusive writable storage during each read.
- **Delayed volume restoration.** The earlier Kotlin close path logged stream-stop timeouts and restored volume immediately. The core ordering test now holds the production session guard across close with an attenuated recording transport. The original volume is restored once the guard drops, and the owner override survives through final native drop. A host probe invokes the compiled AAR's close-policy method through the real JNI library and observes stale-owner rejection. This establishes the binding and the core retirement order separately. Full Android/JNI delayed retirement and acoustic behavior require their own evidence.
- **Scanning and admission.** The scanner resolves the active configuration, scopes device claims to the selected function and checks the format, timing source and packet geometry used by each candidate. The defensive parser gate completed under ASan. The source review traced the principal admission and error paths; some individual transport branches have no dedicated execution test.
- **Powered routing and vendor ledgers.** Discovery scopes one function at a time and aborts after uncertain power restoration. FCP and Gen1 retain each control’s first original value and last verified value. The published vendor gates remain fenced, and Kotlin route hand-back policy now reaches the native owner independently of volume policy.

### Linux USB/IP lifecycle acceptance

The dedicated fixture runs on TCP loopback in a disposable ARM64 Linux guest. The official Alpine `6.12.119-0-lts` kernel provides `vhci_hcd`. The probe uses production usbdac through libusb and Linux usbfs. The fixture design and references are in [its README](../../.github/usb-lifecycle/README.md).

| Case | Observed through Linux usbfs and the driver |
| --- | --- |
| UAC2 clock negotiation | Exact 192 kHz is refused on the 48/96 kHz profile. The same retained owner selects and plays 96 kHz in nearest mode. At alternate 1 the clock reports 96 kHz. |
| UAC2 post-alternate clock failures | A stalled CUR yields `clock_unverified` with an absent reported rate. A stale positive 48 kHz CUR yields the same error with 48 kHz recorded. Both cases leave alternate zero and retire. |
| UAC3 BADD playback | The device selects the separate Speaker configuration. The driver refuses exact 96 kHz, opens and plays 48 kHz PCM16 with explicit feedback, then closes to alternate zero and D2. |
| Full UAC3 powered routing | The first run exposed an IAD admission guard that accepted the reserved subclass `0x00` instead of Full ADC3 `0x01`. The corrected guard accepts the conforming class. The next run observed D0 wake, selector 1→2, readback across power cycles, selector 2→1 at hand-back and final D2. The reported control outcome is `Restored`. |
| Direct full UAC3 rate fallback | The unpowered direct-clock profile advertises 48/96 kHz. Exact 192 kHz is refused. The same owner selects 96 kHz in nearest mode, obtains a fresh post-alternate 96 kHz CUR, plays PCM and retires at alternate zero. |
| UAC2 reconfiguration trace | Live 96→48 kHz replacement closed the original stream, returned one paused 48 kHz stream and later resumed PCM. During the marked replacement phase the fixture observed four alternate-zero writes and one alternate-one activation. Final close restored alternate zero. |
| UAC2 capture isolation | The probe read the expected capture marker bytes through a real IN endpoint. A valid-geometry capture URB failure made capture fail while playback's completed frames continued increasing. The two directions had independent AudioControl functions and direct clocks. |
| Native and JNI delayed retirement | The fixture held a real OUT submission and its unlink reply. Native close timed out with alternate 1 active and retirement pending. In the guest JVM the real JNI owner token also stayed pending and rejected early release. Both probes observed retirement after the unlink reply was released. The native probe confirmed final alternate zero through EP0. |

The kernel's USB/IP transport supplies real URB submission, completion and cancellation. Endpoint cadence in the synthetic model does not represent a physical microframe clock. Testing covers driver interoperability with these declared USB behaviors and the scoped ALSA and USB-IF rules used to construct them.

The JNI case runs the SDK's actual Native declarations and native library in a Linux JVM. Android's platform USB claim and configuration operations retain their separate Kotlin and source-level evidence. Physical-device acceptance is outside this review's scope.

An independent run loaded Alpine's Linux `snd-usb-audio` for the same fixture descriptions. ALSA enumerated S16_LE at 48/96 kHz for UAC2 and direct full UAC3. It enumerated the BADD speaker's S16_LE and S24_3LE alternates at fixed 48 kHz with explicit feedback endpoint `0x81`. ALSA also exercised BADD D1 power control. The comparison checks descriptor interpretation by a second driver. It complements the pinned ALSA source and USB-IF spec comparisons documented in the fixture README.
